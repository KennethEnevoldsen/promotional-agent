## Definition

A communication is in plain language if its wording, structure and design are so clear
that the intended readers can easily **find** what they need, **understand** what they
find, and **use** that information.

## The four governing principles

| Principle | The question it asks | What it means for a 300-character post and its card |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevant** | Does the reader get what they need? | Know who the post is for (people who build or choose embedding models) and include only what they need. This overlaps with "Newsworthy". |
| **Findable** | Can they find it? | The point comes first. The card title states it. Nothing important is held back for part 3 of a thread. |
| **Understandable** | Can they understand it? | Plain words, one idea per sentence, no PR jargon, no exact numbers that don't carry the point. Images (the card) must say the same thing as the text. |
| **Usable** | Can they act on it? | Say what it means for the reader. ISO also asks for evaluation with real readers; for us that is the reviewer reading as a follower before scheduling. |

## Scope

The standard covers printed and digital information that is mainly text, in most written
languages, with English examples. Part 2 (ISO 24495-2) covers legal communication and is
not relevant here.
