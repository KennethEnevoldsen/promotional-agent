## Why it was rejected

Drafted as a pinned first post explaining the account: what it covers, that an agent
drafts from repository activity, that a human reviews, that every number is recomputed.

Three problems, in order of seriousness.

**It would overstate the automation.** Right now this is a person and a chatbot with a
human in the loop at every step — which is simply how people work, and does not need
announcing. A post describing "an agent drafts these" would claim more autonomy than the
thing has. That is an unusual direction to be wrong in, and on an account whose entire
premise is that its claims can be checked, being wrong about *itself* is the worst
available first impression.

**It explains something that has not happened.** A pinned meta-post on an account with no
posts describes a process nobody has seen. The explanation lands better once there is a
feed to explain.

**The profile text already does this job.** What a reader needs before following — what
this covers, who is behind it, that the numbers are checkable, that it does not reply —
fits in a bio. See `profile.md`. A whole post to repeat the bio is a post that says
nothing new.

An earlier version was also mostly process: three posts of rules and disclaimers, when
the thing anyone deciding whether to follow actually wants is a sense of what will land
in their feed.

## Revisit if

**The automation tier changes.** If drafting genuinely becomes agent-led rather than
human-led, that is a real change in what the account is, and it should be disclosed
rather than discovered. That is the moment this post earns its place — and it should be
written then, describing what is actually true then, not resurrected from this draft.

Also worth revisiting if the account ever gets a question often enough that answering it
once, pinned, is better than not answering at all.
